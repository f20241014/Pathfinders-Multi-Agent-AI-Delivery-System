## What is and is not proven

- Shown: on the sampled targets, `h` never exceeded the true free-flow cost-to-go and never broke consistency by more than the tolerance; the rows above say whether any value was below zero at all (see below).
- Shown (only if the traffic model is active): on the sampled edges and grid, traffic never made an edge faster than free-flow; on the sampled trips, `h` never exceeded the time still to drive along the route.
- Some slacks were below zero by tiny amounts, far inside the 1e-06 s tolerance (admissible: 18 (smallest -7.5e-11 s), consistent: 18 (smallest -7.5e-11 s), td: 7 (smallest -1.5e-11 s)). These are counted, not hidden: at that size they look like float rounding, but their cause was not investigated further. A search that compares costs exactly could be affected by such values; D* Lite shrinks the heuristic by `H_SLACK` (`src/search/dstar_lite.py`) for this kind of rounding.
- Not proven by these checks: other targets, other edges or times between grid points. The general argument is in `src/search/README.md` (roads are never shorter than the great-circle line and never faster than the max speed).
- (b2) checks the route `dijkstra_td` found. Because FIFO does not hold exactly (see `docs/fifo_check.md`), that route's remaining time may not be the true fastest time from n, so (b2) on its own is weaker evidence than (a) and (b1).
